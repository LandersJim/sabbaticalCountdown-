📅 Monday, March 23 — Shinjuku & Harajuku ▶
  • Yoyogi Park — Great people-watching, especially Jingu Bridge. A welcome green respite from concrete and crowds. Guide article ↗
  • Meiji Jingu Shrine ↗ (map ↗) — Free, sunrise to sunset. Built 1920. Bow at the large torii gate; stay on either side of the gravel path — the middle is reserved for gods.
  • Coffee at Harajuku Forest Terrace ↗ (opens 9am)
  • Takeshita-dori, Harajuku ↗ — Quirky youth fashion street. A spectacle for about 15 minutes!
  • Daiso (100 yen shop) — Great for souvenirs & gifts.
  • Shibuya Crossing
  • Shibuya Tokyo Foodshow ↗ (10am–9pm) — Upscale grocery and food vendors. Stop BEFORE you're hungry — it can be overwhelming. Front area is touristy; venture to the traditional Japanese counters in the back. Bottom floor mostly savoury, upstairs mostly desserts.
  • Tokyo Government Building — Free observation deck, 9:30am–9:30pm. South tower is less crowded (north 9:30–5pm). Visit at dusk for city lights.

💡 Yoyogi + Meiji Jingu + Takeshita-dori covers the full spectrum of green / traditional / modern Tokyo in 2–3 hours.

📅 Tuesday, March 24 — Akihabara & Ueno ▶
  • Tokyo National Museum ↗ (Tue–Sun 9:30am–5pm) — Most extensive Japanese art collection on the planet. Buddhist sculptures, swords, kimonos, sacred scrolls. Excellent English signage.
  • Ueno Park — Cherry blossoms.
  • Tokyo Cruise Water Bus ↗ — Hamarikyu Gardens → Asakusa (~1 hr scenic ride). Leaving Asakusa at 11:40am or 2:20pm if doing in reverse.
  • Senso-ji Temple ↗ — Tokyo's oldest temple, 645 AD. Main hall 6:30am–5pm, free.
  • Asakusa: Start at Asakusa Culture Center (8F observation deck, opens 9am). Walk Nakamise ↗ tourist street (look; don't eat while walking). Try food vendors but throw away trash where you bought it.
  • Don Quixote — Sensory-overload shopping experience.
  • Tanuki Street — Each lamp has a different tanuki (magical raccoon dog). Rub the belly of the one that brings the luck you want.
  • 🍣 Dinner: Kura Sushi ↗ (conveyor belt) — Make a reservation!

📌 Reminder from Sunday: Make the Kura Sushi reservation before you arrive in Japan.

🍜 Food Guide

A full guide: japan-guide.com Japanese Restaurants ↗

🍣
Sushi
  • Kaiten (conveyor-belt): Easy and fun — take plates from the belt or order via touchscreen, pay by plate color.
  • Classic sushi bars: Sit at the counter, order omakase (chef's choice) for higher quality. Expect higher prices but exceptional fish.
🍱
Classic Set Meals
  • Tonkatsu — Deep-fried breaded pork cutlet; a Tokyo classic. Great for lunch or dinner.
  • Teishoku — Set meal tray with grilled fish, fried items, miso soup, rice, and pickles. Try Ootoya (family favorite).
🍢
Skewers & Grilled
  • Yakitori — Grilled chicken skewers, ideal for an evening with drinks.
  • Izakaya — Great for sharing many small dishes with drinks.
  • Yakiniku — Grill bite-sized beef, pork, and vegetables at the table.
🥢
Tofu & Temple Cuisine
  • Shojin Ryori at Daitoku-ji (Kyoto) — Buddhist vegetarian temple cuisine; slow, contemplative meal.
  • Tofuya Ukai (foot of Tokyo Tower) — Tofu-based kaiseki in a stunning garden setting. Lunch is more reasonable than dinner.
🫕
Hotpot & Communal
  • Shabu-shabu / Sukiyaki — Thinly sliced beef or pork cooked at the table.
  • Nabe — One-pot meals with broth, vegetables, meat/fish. Especially nice in cool weather.
🍜
Casual & Budget
  • Ramen/Udon/Soba — Stand-only station shops are a highlight. Even the cheapest bowl is excellent.
  • Hanamaru Udon — Inexpensive cafeteria-style chain near many JR stations.
  • Konbini (Lawson, 7-Eleven, FamilyMart) — Oden, karaage, onigiri, sandwiches, desserts, coffee. Genuinely good.
🥞
Street-Style & Teppanyaki
  • Okonomiyaki — Japanese savory pancake; an absolute must-try.
  • Yakisoba — Stir-fried noodles, often found alongside okonomiyaki.
  • Hiroshima-yaki — Hiroshima's layered style with noodles added inside. Eat with the small spatula (kote).
🦪
Miyajima Specialties
  • Grilled oysters — Miyajima oysters are among Japan's finest. Eat immediately on Omotesando Street.
  • Eel dishes — Also widely available on the island.
  • Momiji manju — Maple leaf-shaped cakes filled with sweet red bean paste; invented here in 1906.
